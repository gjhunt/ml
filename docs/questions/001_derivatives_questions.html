24.0.1 Question 1

Let \(L:\mathbb{R}^2\to\mathbb{R}\) be \[ L(w_1,w_2)=3w_1^2+2w_1w_2-w_2. \] Compute \(\nabla_wL\) and state its shape.

Answer

The partial derivatives are \[ \frac{\partial L}{\partial w_1}=6w_1+2w_2, \qquad \frac{\partial L}{\partial w_2}=2w_1-1. \] Therefore \[ \nabla_wL= \begin{pmatrix} 6w_1+2w_2\\ 2w_1-1 \end{pmatrix} \in\mathbb{R}^2, \] which has the same shape as \(w\).

24.0.2 Question 2

For fixed \(a,x\in\mathbb{R}^D\) and variable \(w\in\mathbb{R}^D\), compute the gradients of \(a^\top w\) and \(w^\top x\). Justify one identity by expanding it in coordinates.

Answer

The identities are \[ \nabla_w(a^\top w)=a, \qquad \nabla_w(w^\top x)=x. \] For example, \[ a^\top w=\sum_{j=1}^D a_jw_j, \] whose \(k\)th partial derivative is \(a_k\). Stacking the partial derivatives gives \(a\).

24.0.3 Question 3

For fixed \(A\in\mathbb{R}^{D\times D}\), use coordinates to derive \[ \nabla_w(w^\top Aw)=(A+A^\top)w. \] Then state the simplified formula when \(A\) is symmetric.

Answer

Expanding gives \[ w^\top Aw=\sum_i\sum_jw_iA_{ij}w_j. \] Differentiating with respect to \(w_k\) picks up terms with \(i=k\) and terms with \(j=k\): \[ \frac{\partial}{\partial w_k}(w^\top Aw) =\sum_jA_{kj}w_j+\sum_iA_{ik}w_i =(Aw)_k+(A^\top w)_k. \] Hence the gradient is \((A+A^\top)w\). If \(A=A^\top\), it simplifies to \(2Aw\).

24.0.4 Question 4

Diagnose the missing assumption in \[ \nabla_w\left(\frac12w^\top Aw\right)=Aw. \] Give a concrete \(2\times2\) counterexample showing why the assumption is needed.

Answer

The identity requires \(A\) to be symmetric. In general, \[ \nabla_w\left(\frac12w^\top Aw\right) =\frac12(A+A^\top)w. \] Take \[ A=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix}, \qquad w=\begin{pmatrix}u\\v\end{pmatrix}. \] Then \(\frac12w^\top Aw=uv/2\), whose gradient is \((v/2,u/2)^\top\), while \(Aw=(v,0)^\top\). These are not generally equal.

24.0.5 Question 5

Let \(w,a\in\mathbb{R}^D\), \(X\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times D}\), and \(y\in\mathbb{R}^N\). State the shape of each expression:

  1. \(Xw\)
  2. \(y-Xw\)
  3. \(\|y-Xw\|^2\)
  4. \(X^\top(y-Xw)\)

Then explain why \(X(y-Xw)\) is not generally a valid matrix product.

Answer

The shapes are:

  1. \(Xw\in\mathbb{R}^N\).
  2. \(y-Xw\in\mathbb{R}^N\).
  3. \(\|y-Xw\|^2\) is a scalar.
  4. \(X^\top(y-Xw)\in\mathbb{R}^D\) because \(X^\top\) is \(D\times N\).

The product \(X(y-Xw)\) attempts to multiply an \(N\times D\) matrix by an \(N\)-vector. Its inner dimensions are \(D\) and \(N\), which need not agree.

24.0.6 Question 6

Let \(X\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times D}\) and \(y\in\mathbb{R}^N\) be fixed, and define \[ L(w)=\frac12w^\top X^\top Xw-w^\top X^\top y. \] Verify that \(X^\top X\) is symmetric and compute \(\nabla_wL\). Check the shape of your answer.

Answer

Since \[ (X^\top X)^\top=X^\top X, \] the matrix \(X^\top X\) is symmetric. Therefore \[ \nabla_w\left(\frac12w^\top X^\top Xw\right)=X^\top Xw. \] The second term is linear in \(w\), with gradient \(-X^\top y\). Hence \[ \nabla_wL=X^\top Xw-X^\top y=X^\top(Xw-y). \] This is a \(D\)-vector, matching the shape of \(w\).

24.0.7 Question 7

Apply the scalar-intermediate chain rule to \[ L(w)=\frac12(a^\top w-b)^2, \] where \(a,w\in\mathbb{R}^D\) and \(b\in\mathbb{R}\). Identify a scalar intermediate \(s(w)\) and an outer function \(\ell(s)\) such that \(L(w)=\ell(s(w))\), then derive \(\nabla_wL(w)\).

Answer

Let \[ s(w)=a^\top w-b, \qquad \ell(s)=\frac12s^2. \] Then \(L(w)=\ell(s(w))\). Since \[ \frac{d\ell}{ds}=s, \qquad \nabla_ws(w)=a, \] the chain rule gives \[ \nabla_wL(w)=\frac{d\ell}{ds}\nabla_ws(w) =(a^\top w-b)a. \]

24.0.8 Question 8

Starting from \[ \sigma(s)=\frac{1}{1+e^{-s}}, \] compute \(d\sigma/ds\) and express the result only in terms of \(p=\sigma(s)\).

Answer

Direct differentiation gives \[ \frac{d\sigma}{ds}=\frac{e^{-s}}{(1+e^{-s})^2}. \] Since \[ p=\frac1{1+e^{-s}}, \qquad 1-p=\frac{e^{-s}}{1+e^{-s}}, \] we obtain \[ \frac{d\sigma}{ds}=p(1-p). \]

24.0.9 Question 9

For fixed \(x\) and \(y\in\{0,1\}\), define \[ s(w)=x^\top w, \qquad p(w)=\sigma(s(w)), \] and let \[ \ell_y(p)=-\left[y\log p+(1-y)\log(1-p)\right], \qquad L(w)=\ell_y(p(w)). \] Use the chain rule through \(p\) and \(s\) to derive \(\nabla_wL(w)=(p(w)-y)x\). Display the three derivative factors before simplifying.

Answer

Writing \(s=s(w)\) and \(p=p(w)\), the three factors are \[ \frac{d\ell_y}{dp} =-\frac{y}{p}+\frac{1-y}{1-p} =\frac{p-y}{p(1-p)}, \] \[ \frac{dp}{ds}=p(1-p), \qquad \nabla_ws(w)=x. \] Therefore \[ \nabla_wL(w) =\frac{d\ell_y}{dp} \frac{dp}{ds}\nabla_ws(w) =(p-y)x. \]

24.0.10 Question 10

Let \[ z_1(w)=w_1+w_2, \qquad z_2(w)=w_1w_2, \] and define \[ \ell(z)=\frac12(z_1^2+z_2^2), \qquad L(w)=\ell(z(w)). \] Use \[ \frac{\partial L}{\partial w_j} =\sum_{k=1}^2 \frac{\partial \ell}{\partial z_k} \frac{\partial z_k}{\partial w_j} \] to compute \(\nabla_wL\) at \(w=(1,2)^\top\).

Answer

At \(w=(1,2)^\top\), \((z_1,z_2)=(3,2)\) and \[ \frac{\partial\ell}{\partial z_1}=z_1, \qquad \frac{\partial\ell}{\partial z_2}=z_2. \] Thus \[ \frac{\partial L}{\partial w_1} =z_1(1)+z_2(w_2)=3+4=7, \] and \[ \frac{\partial L}{\partial w_2} =z_1(1)+z_2(w_1)=3+2=5. \] Therefore \[ \nabla_wL= \begin{pmatrix}7\\5\end{pmatrix}. \]

24.0.11 Question 11

Let \(W\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times2}\) be variable, let \[ B=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\2&3\end{pmatrix}, \] and define \[ L(W)=\frac12\sum_{d=1}^2\sum_{k=1}^2(W_{dk}-B_{dk})^2. \] Derive \(\nabla_WL\). Then evaluate both \(L(W)\) and \(\nabla_WL\) at \[ W=\begin{pmatrix}2&-1\\0&4\end{pmatrix}. \]

Answer

For each entry, \[ \frac{\partial L}{\partial W_{dk}}=W_{dk}-B_{dk}, \] so \(\nabla_WL=W-B\). At the specified matrix, \[ W-B= \begin{pmatrix} 1&-2\\ -2&1 \end{pmatrix}. \] Therefore \[ L(W)=\frac12(1^2+(-2)^2+(-2)^2+1^2)=5 \] and \[ \nabla_WL= \begin{pmatrix} 1&-2\\ -2&1 \end{pmatrix}. \]

24.0.12 Question 12

Write \[ W=\begin{pmatrix}u&v\\r&s\end{pmatrix} \] and define \[ L(W)=uv+(r-s)^2. \] Compute the four partial derivatives and arrange them in the \(2\times2\) matrix \(\nabla_WL\).

Answer

The partial derivatives are \[ \frac{\partial L}{\partial u}=v, \qquad \frac{\partial L}{\partial v}=u, \] \[ \frac{\partial L}{\partial r}=2(r-s), \qquad \frac{\partial L}{\partial s}=-2(r-s). \] Placing each derivative in the position of its corresponding entry gives \[ \nabla_WL= \begin{pmatrix} v&u\\ 2(r-s)&-2(r-s) \end{pmatrix}. \]
